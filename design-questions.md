# The design side: what I need from you

The engineering dimension is written for the six tier-one projects, from the repositories on
this machine. The design dimension cannot be written that way, because no repository records who
you spoke to, what you tried, or what you rejected. These are three questions per project, chosen
against what each page already argues so the answers slot in rather than starting a new section
from nothing.

Short answers are fine. A sentence each is enough to write from.

---

## AMMBAN — lead brand designer
The page argues that 200+ assets is a systems problem rather than a logo project.
1. Who briefed you, and what did they ask for that turned out to be the wrong thing to ask for?
2. What did you look at before starting: other associations, the existing materials, agents themselves?
3. What did you decide once that saved you the most work later across the asset set?

## Brand Applied — brand designer
The page argues identity survives or dies when it hits apparel, embroidery and uniform.
1. Which specific job taught you the embroidery lesson, and what broke?
2. Did you test anything physically before committing, a sample, a print proof, a stitch-out?
3. What does a logo need for you to consider it applied-ready now that you did not check before?

## Moniepoint, South West — brand and identity designer
The page argues that working inside someone else's brand is its own discipline.
1. What was the actual remit, and who decided what you could and could not change?
2. What did the region need that head office material did not supply, and how did you find that out?
3. Did anything you made get adopted more widely, or stay regional?

## Print & Editorial — designer
The page argues print does not forgive and a cover is a single-frame argument.
1. Pick one cover. What was the manuscript about and what did you decide the frame should be?
2. Did you get it wrong on press at any point, and what did that cost?
3. Who were you designing the covers for, the author, the publisher, or the shelf?

## JAYE — lead product designer, brand and identity
The summary already claims competitive analysis and research. The page does not show it.
1. What did the competitive analysis actually find, and what did it stop you from building?
2. Who did you talk to or observe, and what surprised you about how people choose a night out?
3. What did you prototype and then kill?

## YarnDok — product designer, brand and front end
The page argues medical hierarchy and accessibility are not aesthetic problems.
1. What did you use to decide the hierarchy: clinical guidance, a practitioner, existing products?
2. What accessibility decision cost you something visually, and why was it still right?
3. You took it through to code. What did building it teach you that the design did not?

## Fresh Pick Mart — brand identity designer
The page argues retail identity gets tested in three dimensions and the style guide is the deliverable.
1. What is the legal component on the packaging, and did it change the design?
2. Did you see it in the shop after launch, and did it hold up?
3. It is called early work. What would you do differently now?

## Microsoft AgroTech Hackathon — brand and visual designer
The page argues an event has a deadline that does not move.
1. How long did you actually have, and what did you cut to make it?
2. What was produced at scale, and what nearly went wrong in production?
3. What did the social package achieve, in whatever terms you have?
